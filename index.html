HOW YOUR WORLD WORKS

A small model of a very big system.

Pelagos is a deterministic, Earth-sized worldbuilding sandbox. It helps compare scenarios, but is not a validated geophysical or weather forecast model.

Moving plates

Seeded spherical Voronoi plates rotate about fixed Euler poles. Relative velocities classify convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries. Terrain moves with each plate. Accumulated collision builds fold mountains, subduction trenches, and volcanic arcs; divergence creates spreading ridges and rifts. Three historical samples carry earlier formation belts with their plates. Mechanical uplift is independent of volcanic activity. Islands are connected land cells, with ocean wraparound.

Climate estimates

Latitude, elevation, ocean influence, greenhouse warming, axial tilt, and rotation drive approximate temperature and rainfall. Geological time is in millions of years; the weather display represents long-term climate, not individual storms. Seasons shows estimated annual temperature range. Planet age affects cooling and volcanic activity.

Rivers, erosion & currents

Rainfall produces estimated runoff routed to the steepest lower neighbor, including diagonals. Closed depressions retain water and sediment rather than being forcibly drained. Erosion grows with elapsed geological time, slope, runoff, and your erosion setting; removed sediment is deposited downstream or in coastal shelf fans. Channel lines are exaggerated for visibility, and rivers smaller than the grid are omitted. Surface currents illustrate latitude-dependent winds and regional circulation, steering around coastlines. Their animation has its own visual clock and continues while geological time is paused. Current strength and day length affect the flow illustration. Currents do not yet transport heat into the climate model; this is not an ocean-fluid solver.

Limits to keep in mind

Formation heights use heuristic growth from accumulated closure and a three-sample history, rather than a full rock-deformation solver. Plate-scale crust buoyancy is seeded. The Uplift layer shows deformation relative to advected procedural terrain, not total elevation change since the initial map. Plates retain their identities: this model does not conserve crust mass or solve mantle convection or full atmospheric and ocean fluid dynamics. Terrain at plate edges may change abruptly. Island counts depend on grid resolution (960 × 480), so tiny islands are omitted. The map uses an equirectangular projection; its scale varies with latitude. Coverage and averages use spherical area weights.
